\begin{abstract}
\pagenumbering{gobble}

Pancreas transplantation restores normoglycaemia in patients with type~1 diabetes, but graft rejection remains a common complication that threatens long-term outcomes. The reference standard for diagnosis is percutaneous biopsy, an invasive procedure with inherent risks and sampling limitations. Recent work has shown that clinical ultrasound biomarkers---Acoustic Radiation Force Impulse (ARFI) elastography and dynamic contrast-enhanced ultrasound (DCE-US)---can discriminate rejection non-invasively in the late post-transplant period. However, these techniques require specialised acquisition protocols and manual measurement. Radiomics, the automated extraction of quantitative texture features from medical images, offers a potentially scalable alternative.

This thesis investigates whether radiomics texture features extracted from conventional grayscale ultrasound images can predict pancreas transplant rejection. A dataset of 137 ultrasound studies from 55 patients (39 studies with clinically diagnosed rejection, 27 of which were biopsy-confirmed) was analysed. The preprocessing pipeline isolated the pancreas graft from clinician-annotated images, and 93 standardised features were extracted using PyRadiomics. To respect the repeated-measures structure of the cohort, the primary statistical testing (Mann-Whitney U and Welch's $t$-test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction) and machine learning classification (logistic regression, random forest, SVM, and naive Bayes within a joint optimisation framework) were performed on an independent dataset with one study per patient. Robustness was further assessed with paired within-patient comparisons, alternative texture features (Local Binary Patterns, Gabor filters, and Laws' texture energy, 153 features in total), and surrounding tissue normalisation. The full dataset was analysed only as an exploratory supplement.

No radiomics feature discriminated rejection after multiple testing correction, and machine learning classifiers achieved AUC values indistinguishable from chance (best: 0.636, 95\% CI [0.48, 0.78]). As a positive control, the analysis pipeline reproduced the published clinical-biomarker result of Bassaganyas et al., confirming that ARFI elastography significantly discriminates rejection in the late post-transplant period ($p < 0.001$, effect size $r = 0.72$), with values matching the published analysis to three decimal places. Both a paired within-patient analysis ($p = 0.86$) and the independent-dataset clinical analysis indicated that this ARFI signal reflects between-patient, late-period differences in baseline stiffness rather than a transient rejection-induced change detectable across the cohort.

Automated texture analysis of grayscale ultrasound does not capture the tissue changes associated with pancreas transplant rejection. Clinical biomarkers measuring tissue stiffness and perfusion remain necessary for non-invasive rejection monitoring. Future work should explore deep learning approaches, multi-parametric imaging models, and larger multi-centre cohorts.
